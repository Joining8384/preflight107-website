## Why 250 grams is the magic number

The FAA sets a weight floor for drone registration at **0.55 lbs — about 250 grams**. Manufacturers know it, which is why so many popular drones land at 249g: it's a deliberate design target to sit just under the line. But the line only matters in one specific context, and that's where most people get tripped up.

The real answer to "do I need to register?" isn't about weight first. **It's about why you're flying.**

## The question that actually decides it: recreational or Part 107?

The FAA splits all drone flying into two lanes, and registration works differently in each.

### Flying recreationally (for fun)

If you fly **strictly for recreation** and your drone is **under 250g**, you do **not** need to register it with the FAA. This is the exemption the DJI Mini and similar drones are built around.

But "recreational" is stricter than it sounds. The moment your flight has any business purpose — a photo you sell, footage for a client, real estate, an inspection, *anything* that isn't pure personal enjoyment — you are no longer recreational, and the exemption disappears.

### Flying under Part 107 (commercial)

If you fly under **Part 107** — the rules for any commercial or non-recreational operation — you must **register your drone regardless of weight.** A 249g DJI Mini flown for a paid job needs the same $5 FAA registration as a two-kilo Mavic. There is no sub-250g exemption on the Part 107 side. None.

That single distinction is the whole ballgame. Same drone, same weight — the registration answer flips entirely based on the purpose of the flight.

## Don't confuse registration with TRUST

Here's a trap even careful pilots fall into: sub-250g recreational flyers skip registration and assume they've skipped *all* paperwork. They haven't.

**Every recreational flyer, at any weight, must pass TRUST** — The Recreational UAS Safety Test. It's free, it takes a few minutes online, and you have to carry proof of completion. A featherweight drone doesn't exempt you from it.

So the recreational sub-250g pilot's actual checklist is: no registration, **but yes, TRUST.**

## What about Remote ID?

Remote ID — the "digital license plate" that broadcasts your drone's location and identity — follows the registration rule, which makes sub-250g a genuine break here:

- **Sub-250g, flown recreationally (not registered):** exempt from Remote ID. You don't have to broadcast.
- **Sub-250g, flown under Part 107 (registered):** because the drone must be registered, it must **comply with Remote ID** like any other Part 107 aircraft.

Again, it's the *why*, not just the *weight*, that decides.

## The rules that apply no matter how light your drone is

This is the part the "under 250g = no rules" crowd gets dangerously wrong. Weight exempts you from **registration** (when recreational) and **Remote ID** (when recreational). It exempts you from almost nothing else. A 249g drone still has to follow:

- **Airspace rules.** You still can't fly in controlled airspace around an airport without authorization. **LAANC** approval applies to your Mini exactly like it applies to everyone else.
- **Altitude and line of sight.** 400 feet AGL, keep it within visual line of sight, don't fly over people carelessly.
- **TFRs and restricted airspace.** Temporary flight restrictions, stadiums, national security areas — a light drone is just as prohibited as a heavy one.
- **No careless or reckless operation.** Flying into clouds, over crowds, or near manned aircraft is illegal at any weight.

The FAA cares about *where and how* a sub-250g drone flies just as much as a large one. The weight break is about paperwork, not permission to ignore the airspace.

## So, do you need to register?

Put it all together and it's a two-step check:

1. **Are you flying for any reason other than pure fun?** → Part 107 → **register, regardless of weight.**
2. **Purely recreational, and under 250g?** → **no registration**, but you still need **TRUST**, and every airspace rule still applies.

## Bottom line

The 250-gram line is real, but it only buys you out of **registration and Remote ID, and only when you're flying purely recreationally.** Fly that same tiny drone for money and you're squarely under Part 107 — register it. And no matter which lane you're in, the airspace rules don't get lighter just because your drone did.
