## The Most Common Part 107 Misunderstanding

Ask ten certified drone pilots when their Part 107 "expires" and you'll get a mess of answers: two years, five years, never, "I think I have to retake the test?"

Here's the clean version: **your Remote Pilot Certificate does not expire.** The plastic card is yours. What *does* lapse is your **currency** — the requirement to complete recurrent training every 24 calendar months. Let that slide and your certificate is still valid, but you are **not legally allowed to fly commercially** until you catch it up.

That distinction matters, because a lot of pilots either panic about a nonexistent expiration date or, worse, assume they're fine and quietly fly out of currency for months.

## Certificate vs. Currency

Think of it like this:

- **Your certificate** is the credential — proof you passed the Aeronautical Knowledge Test. It doesn't have an expiration date printed on it, and you never lose it (barring FAA action).
- **Your currency** is the permission to *exercise* that certificate. It runs on a **24-calendar-month clock**, and you reset it by completing recurrent training.

So the honest answer to "does my Part 107 expire?" is: *no, but your ability to use it does.*

The "calendar month" part matters — currency runs to the **end** of the 24th month, not the exact anniversary date. If you completed training on March 12th, you're covered through the end of March two years later.

## The Good News: No More In-Person Test

If you got certified before 2021, you remember the old system: every two years, back to a testing center, pay the fee, take a proctored exam again. That's **gone.**

Recurrent training is now a **free online course** through the FAA Safety Team at **FAASafety.gov**. You create an account, take the training module, work through the knowledge checks, and get a completion certificate. Most pilots finish it in **under an hour**, at home, for **zero dollars**.

There's no proctor, no testing center, no fee, and no way to "fail" it in the traditional sense — the knowledge checks walk you until you get them right. The FAA's goal here is refreshing your knowledge, not gatekeeping.

## What's Actually Covered

The recurrent module refreshes the core Part 107 material: regulations, airspace classification and operating requirements, weather effects on performance, loading and performance, and operational safety — plus the **night operations** content that became part of the standard curriculum when the night rules changed. If you got your original certificate before those night rules took effect, this training is what qualifies you to fly at night without a waiver.

## Keep the Completion Certificate

When you finish, you get a **completion certificate — save it.** Part 107 requires you to make your certificate and evidence of training available to the FAA on request, so a screenshot buried in your camera roll two phones ago isn't a plan. Keep a PDF somewhere durable and, ideally, with your other flight documents.

## What Happens If You Let It Lapse

Nothing dramatic happens automatically — no one revokes anything, and you won't get a letter. But the moment your 24 months are up:

- You **cannot legally conduct Part 107 operations** until you complete the training.
- If you fly commercially while out of currency, you're operating in violation — and that's the kind of thing that surfaces at exactly the wrong moment, like after an incident or during an FAA inquiry.
- Your **insurance and client contracts** may also assume you're current. Flying lapsed could complicate a claim.

The fix is simple: complete the training and you're immediately current again. There's no penalty period or waiting. But you can't retroactively cover flights you made while lapsed.

## A Note for Part 61 Pilots

If you hold a manned-aircraft pilot certificate under Part 61 and have a current flight review, you have a **different recurrent path** — you complete the FAA's small-UAS training designed for Part 61 holders instead. Same idea, different course.

## The Habit That Prevents All of This

The reason pilots lapse isn't laziness — it's that **24 months is too long to remember anything.** You take the training, feel responsible, and then two years of life happen.

Two things fix it:
1. **Do it early.** You can complete recurrent training before your 24 months are up — there's no penalty for being ahead, and it resets your clock. Don't wait for month 24.
2. **Set a reminder that will actually reach you** — not a mental note. Calendar it, or use something that tracks the deadline for you.

## The Bottom Line

Your **Part 107 certificate never expires** — but your **currency lapses every 24 calendar months**, and without it you can't legally fly commercially. The renewal is **free online training** through FAASafety.gov, takes about an hour, and there's no test center and no fee. Do it early rather than at the deadline, save the completion certificate, and set a reminder you'll actually see — because the only real risk here is forgetting.
