Where Can You Legally Fly a Drone? The Off-Limits Places the Airspace Map Won't Show You

You checked the airspace, it's clear Class G, no TFRs — so you're good to fly, right? Not necessarily. The FAA controls the airspace, but a completely separate patchwork of rules — national parks, security zones, state and local laws — controls many of the actual places you'd want to launch from, and almost none of them show up on an airspace map. Here's the full picture: the places that are flat-out banned, the ones that need permission, the local-law trap most pilots never think about, and how to actually know before you drive out.
